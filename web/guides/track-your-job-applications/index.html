How to track your job applications

After the tenth application, memory fails: which version of the résumé went where, who promised to call back, which postings were duplicates. A small amount of tracking turns a job search from a series of guesses into something you can improve week by week.

The stages that matter

Fewer, clearer stages beat a long list you will not maintain. These six cover almost every process:

StageMeaning
SavedA posting worth applying to; nothing sent yet.
ReadyTailored résumé, cover letter and answers prepared and reviewed.
AppliedSubmitted. Record the date; every follow-up is counted from here.
ScreeningAny first contact: recruiter call, take-home test, questionnaire.
InterviewConversations with the hiring team, however many rounds.
OfferA written offer. Closed stages: Rejected and Withdrawn.

Keep the date of each change. The dates are what later tell you how long each company takes and whether a silence is normal or final.

What to record for each application

  • Company, role title, location or remote policy, and the posting link (the posting often disappears later).
  • Which profile or résumé version you sent, so an interviewer's questions match what they read.
  • The source: a job board, a referral, a recruiter, the company's own site. Sources differ enormously in response rate.
  • Names and contact details of anyone you spoke to, and what they said about timing.
  • The next action and its date: follow up, prepare, send documents.

When to follow up

If you have heard nothing seven to ten days after applying, one short, polite message restating your interest and availability is reasonable; after an interview, a thank-you the same day and a check-in when the promised date passes. More than that rarely helps. Your tracker's dates make this automatic: anything in Applied for more than ten days with no next action is a follow-up candidate.

Read your numbers

Two ratios tell most of the story. Response rate is the share of applications that reached Screening or further; if it is low, the résumé and targeting need work (see tailoring your résumé to the job description). Interview conversion is the share of screenings that became interviews, and of interviews that became offers; if those are low, the preparation and the stories need work. Break the numbers down by source and by profile: a 3% response rate from a job board and 30% from referrals is a clear instruction about where to spend your time.

A weekly review

  1. Close what is dead

    Move stale applications to Rejected when a reasonable time has passed with no answer. A clean pipeline shows you the real picture.

  2. Do the follow-ups

    Everything past its follow-up date gets one message or gets closed.

  3. Look at the ratios

    Response rate by source, conversion by stage. Decide one change for next week: a different source, a tighter target role, a rewritten summary.

  4. Fill the top

    Save the next batch of postings worth applying to, so the week starts with work ready to go.
